SYNTHETIC DEMONSTRATION TRANSCRIPT
This text was written for demonstrating SCAMVERSE. It is not a real interview and
does not describe real persons, cases or organisations.

Interviewer: How do victims usually first come into contact with the scheme?
Officer A: Most of them see an advertisement on Facebook or receive a link in a WhatsApp group. The link brings them to a Telegram group where an "agent" answers questions all day. The group looks busy because many members are fake accounts posting screenshots of profit.

Interviewer: What do the offenders promise?
Officer A: A guaranteed return, usually 30% in a few weeks, and they say there is no risk. They show a fake company certificate and claim to be registered with SSM. Some victims are told the slot is limited and they must act now, within 3 hours, or lose the bonus.

Interviewer: Who are the victims in the cases you handle?
Officer B: Many are retirees and teachers, but also young professionals. Some are lecturers and nurses. Greed is part of it, but trust in the testimonial and the receipt they are shown is usually what convinces them.

Interviewer: How does the money move after the transfer?
Officer B: The victim transfers money to a mule account at a local bank. Within minutes the funds are split and transferred again through several layers of accounts, withdrawn at an ATM, or converted to crypto and sent to a wallet overseas. Sometimes the TAC is obtained from the account holder who rented out the account.

Interviewer: What happens once a police report is lodged?
Officer C: We record the statement, collect the evidence such as the receipt and chat records, and open an investigation under Section 420. We work with NSRC to request that the bank freeze the account, and with BNM and SSM to verify whether the company is licensed. When the mule is traced there may be an arrest and a charge, but the mastermind is often outside the country, so many files end as NFA.

Interviewer: What would improve prevention?
Officer C: Awareness campaigns that teach people to check the BNM alert list, a single hotline, and faster coordination between the police, banks, SKMM and the platforms. Education in schools and seminars for older people would help, together with a mule check before any transfer.
